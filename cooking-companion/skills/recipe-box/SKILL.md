# Recipe box: find, save and recall

## Finding recipes

1. Work out what they want: a dish ("chicken thighs"), a vibe ("cosy and quick"), an occasion ("dinner party for 6") or an ingredient to use up. Fill gaps from `kitchen-profile.md` (time, equipment, skill, tastes) instead of asking.
2. **Check the recipe box first.** A saved recipe rated 4 or higher that fits is often the best answer: "You gave the miso salmon a 5. Want that again?"
3. Then search, starting with the sites in `sources.md`. Prefer tested recipes with weights and clear timings.
4. Run every candidate through **dietary-guard**.
5. Offer **two or three options**. For each, give a one-line pitch, the total time, any dietary-guard note, and the link. Don't paste the full recipe until they pick one.

## When they pick one

Show the ingredients (scaled to their default servings) and a short method in your own words, then offer recipe-adapter tweaks ("Want it dairy-free, or in the air fryer?") and cook-along mode.

## Saving

- Save to `recipe-box.md` when they ask to, or after they say they cooked it and liked it. Use the entry template in the file.
- Store the **source link, the ingredients, and a short method in your own words**, plus their tweaks. Don't copy the author's write-up.
- Record who it passed dietary-guard for.
- Family recipes or ones the user dictates are theirs, so save them verbatim.

## Ratings and notes

- After they cook something, ask in one line: "How was it, 1 to 5? Anything you'd change?" Update the rating, the last-cooked date and the notes.
- Apply their notes automatically next time ("I've halved the chili like you said last time").
- Use ratings to shape future suggestions: more like the 5s, less like the 1s and 2s.

## Recall

Answer things like "What was that lentil thing we made?" or "Show my freezer-friendly recipes" from `recipe-box.md`, searching the names, tags and notes.
